Meeting people online means agreeing to show yourself. But showing **a little** isn't showing **everything**. Between total anonymity, which attracts no one, and full exposure, which leaves you open to unpleasant situations, there is a middle ground that everyone sets in their own way.

Here is what is visible on your profile, what isn't, and the settings that let you adjust your discretion to your situation and your comfort.

## What other people see about you

When someone opens your profile, they can see:

- your **first name or nickname** and your **age** (not your date of birth);
- your **city and country**;
- your **photos**, your **bio**, your **answers to questions**, your **interests** and **values**;
- the lifestyle details you have chosen to fill in (height, children, habits…);
- your **verified badge**, if you have earned it;
- the **month you joined** ("member since…"), never the exact day.

They **never see** your email address, your phone number, your full date of birth or your home address. Your email appears nowhere on your profile.

Some sensitive information, such as **religion**, is shown only if you have decided to make it visible: it is a choice, not a default.

## Location: approximate, never precise

If you choose to, you can share an **approximate location**, which is used to calculate the distance between you and other members. This distance is **rounded** (in steps of a few kilometres): it can't be used to pinpoint you. If you share nothing, the distance may be estimated from your city when it is known.

If this bothers you, don't share your location: you will keep using the app, just with less precision about who is nearby.

## Activity status

The people you have a duo with can see an approximate status: "online", "active today", "active this week". It **never shows the exact time** you were last online. Depending on your plan, you may also see this status on profiles that aren't your duos yet.

This status works both ways: if you hide yours, you won't see other people's either, except with certain plans. You can hide it in your settings if you prefer people not to know when you are around.

## Profile visitors

The people who view your profile are counted. Depending on your plan, you see only **how many** people stopped by, or the **list** of who they are. It works both ways: when you visit a profile, that person may notice if their plan allows it.

If this two-way visibility bothers you, **Incognito mode**, available with certain plans, lets you view profiles without showing up among their visitors.

## Incognito mode

Incognito mode is designed for people who want more discretion: a public figure, a professional worried about being recognised, someone coming out of a difficult situation. It hides your activity status and stops your profile visits from being recorded. It doesn't make you invisible: your profile is still shown to others according to your filters, and you can still like, chat and meet.

## Pause mode and quiet hours

Two other settings affect your discretion and your peace of mind:

- **Pause mode** hides you from Discover and suspends incoming likes and alerts, while keeping your conversations and your duos. You can come back whenever you want.
- **Quiet hours** let you set times when you don't want to be notified, at night for example. Notifications are kept in the app but don't ring.

Both settings help protect your energy and your privacy, without deleting anything.

## What you control in your conversations

- **You can delete** your messages.
- **You can block** someone: they disappear from your Discover, and you from theirs.
- **You can report** problematic behaviour, with the option of attaching the latest messages as evidence **only if you agree to it**.
- **You stay in control** of what you share: photos, information, contact details.

## A few personal safety rules

Beyond the settings, your own habits matter most:

- **Don't give out your contact details too early** (phone number, address, workplace).
- **Avoid photos that reveal where you are**: a building front, a number plate, a uniform.
- **Use photos you don't post everywhere**, because a photo used on other sites can lead back to you through an image search.
- **Stay vague about your schedule and routines** at first.
- **Keep the conversation on the platform** until trust is established.

## Your data and your right to decide

You also keep rights over your data: you can export what the app holds about you, or delete your account. These options are in your settings. They exist so that your trust is never a trap: you can leave whenever you decide to.

## A simple habit: put yourself in a visitor's shoes

To judge your level of discretion, open your profile the way a stranger would see it. What do they learn about your life? Could they guess where you work, which neighbourhood you live in, what your routine looks like? If so, adjust: remove a photo that is too recognisable, rephrase a sentence that is too specific. This twenty-second read, done once a quarter, is worth all the settings in the world.

## Finding the right level for you

There is no single right setting for everyone. A few pointers to help you choose:

- **If you are just starting out**, begin with a simple profile, without a precise location, and open up more as trust grows.
- **If you are well known in your circle** (community, profession), Incognito mode and a careful bio can spare you awkward situations.
- **If you want to meet a wide range of people**, a more open profile attracts more interest, with the same safety rules.

> Discretion isn't distrust: it is the freedom to show what you want, to whom you want, when you want.

Take ten minutes to review your privacy settings. That little time will spare you plenty of surprises, and let you approach dating with the calm of someone who knows exactly what they are showing.
