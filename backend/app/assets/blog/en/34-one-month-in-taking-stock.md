After a few weeks on a dating app, one question starts to nag: "Is this working?" You count your Likes, compare notes with friends, worry about a silence and wonder whether you are doing something wrong. Without any reference points, all that questioning quickly turns into discouragement.

Taking stock after a month is not about judging yourself. It is a way to **take back control**: understand what happened, adjust what can be adjusted, and decide calmly what comes next.

## Why a month?

A week is too short: the first few days are often unusual (curiosity, novelty, a profile you are still fine-tuning). Three months is a long time to keep going without knowing whether you are getting anywhere. A month gives you time to see the first conversations, the first silences and the beginnings of a rhythm, while staying short enough to correct course without losing momentum.

## The numbers that help, and the ones that mislead

### Worth watching

- **The number of real conversations**: the ones where you exchanged more than a few messages. They measure interest better than Likes do.
- **Your reply rate**: out of ten people you wrote to, how many answered? A very low rate may point to a problem with your first message.
- **The dates or calls** you have arranged. This is the measure closest to your actual goal.
- **How it felt**: after a conversation, did you feel good, bored, uneasy? Your feelings count as much as the figures.

### Worth keeping in perspective

- **The raw number of Likes received.** It depends on your city, the time of year and how many members there are. It says nothing about whether those Likes suit you.
- **The compatibility percentage** as a verdict on yourself: it describes how close two profiles are, not what you are worth.
- **Comparing yourself with others.** Every city, every age group and every situation has its own dynamic.

## Taking stock in four questions

Set aside half an hour, grab a notebook and answer honestly:

1. **What went well?** A pleasant conversation, someone you met, a moment you enjoyed.
2. **What weighed on me?** The time spent, the disappointment of a silence, someone who was too pushy, the feeling of repeating myself.
3. **What have I not tried yet?** A call, a Like with a short note, a Duo Night, a different set of photos.
4. **What do I really want for next month?** To meet people, to chat, to take a break, to try something else.

Your answers will often hand you the decision before you even start analysing them.

## If you have had few reactions

A quiet month is not a disaster. Before concluding that "it doesn't work", go through the most common causes:

- **An incomplete profile.** Too few photos or unclear ones, an empty bio, hardly any interests.
- **Photos that don't show your face clearly.** Sunglasses, harsh backlighting, group shots where nobody can tell which one is you.
- **Filters that are too tight.** A narrow age range, a single city, a long list of criteria.
- **First messages that are too generic.** A plain "Hi" gets few replies; a message that picks up on a detail gets many more.
- **An irregular rhythm.** Showing up only now and then reduces your visibility and how quickly you can respond.
- **A city with few members.** In cities that are still growing, you sometimes have to wait for new members to arrive.

Fix one thing at a time, then watch what happens: changing ten things at once makes it impossible to know what worked.

## If you have had plenty of conversations but little progress

This is another common situation: lots of chatting, very few meetings. A few ideas:

- **Suggest a call sooner**, after a few days of pleasant messages.
- **Be clear about what you are looking for**, so you attract people on the same wavelength.
- **Close conversations that are going nowhere**, politely, rather than letting them fizzle out: it frees up energy.
- **Limit how many conversations you run at once.** Three conversations you actually follow are worth more than fifteen patchy ones.

## If you have met someone

Congratulations, but taking stock is still useful: how do you feel after your dates? Does this person respect your pace? Do they speak clearly about what they want? Keep your bearings, and don't let the excitement of the early days make you drop your guard: give things time.

## Deciding what's next: three options

### Carry on and adjust

If you have had at least a few good moments and feel like trying again, carry on with one or two specific changes (photos, first message, rhythm).

### Take a break

If you are tired, discouraged or going through a busy period, put the app on pause. It is an active, healthy decision: you keep your conversations and come back whenever you like.

### Stop

Sometimes the app simply isn't right for what you are looking for, or you have met someone: closing your account is a legitimate choice, not a failure. You can export your data or delete your account from your settings.

## Avoiding the dependency trap

A few signs that the app is taking up too much room in your life:

- you check your notifications every time you have a spare moment;
- your mood depends on how many reactions you get;
- you keep using the app even though you no longer want to;
- you neglect your friends, your work or your sleep.

If so, cut back to two or three short visits a week, turn off non-essential notifications, or take a proper break. Meeting someone matters, but not at the cost of your wellbeing.

## A month, not a verdict

> A month without results says nothing about your worth. It only says something about your profile, your rhythm and your timing.

Take stock with the same kindness you would show a friend: notice, adjust, decide. You took a first step by signing up; the second is learning from this first month. And if the third is setting the app aside for a while, that will still be a good choice, because it will be yours.
