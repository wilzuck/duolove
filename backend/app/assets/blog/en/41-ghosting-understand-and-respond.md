You had been chatting for several days. The messages were regular, you laughed together, you even talked about meeting. Then, overnight, nothing: no reply, no explanation, just silence. This has a name: **ghosting**, disappearing like a ghost, without warning.

If it has happened to you, you are neither alone nor foolish. It is one of the most confusing experiences in online dating, precisely because it leaves every question open. This guide helps you understand what is going on, respond without hurting yourself, and avoid repeating the pattern in turn.

## Why some people disappear

The other person's silence rarely says anything about you. It mostly says something about their situation, their fears or their habits. The most common reasons:

- **Fear of confrontation.** Saying "I don't want to continue" takes a bit of courage that not everyone has. Going quiet feels easier, even if it isn't kinder.
- **A change in circumstances.** Something developing with someone else, getting back with an ex, family pressure, a bereavement, a move.
- **App fatigue.** Some people open lots of conversations at once, then drop all of them at the same time.
- **A loss of interest they can't own up to.** The spark faded, for no particular reason, and they don't know how to put it into words.
- **Everyday constraints.** A demanding job, an unreliable network, a shared phone, exam season: sometimes the silence isn't even a final decision.

None of these reasons makes ghosting pleasant. But they remind you of something essential: **this silence is not a judgement on your worth**.

## Don't take it personally

Faced with silence, the mind looks for an explanation, and it often finds one that turns against you: "I wrote too much", "They didn't like my photo", "I'm not interesting enough". These thoughts are normal, but they are rarely accurate.

A few pointers to keep a cool head:

- **You only knew a small part of this person.** A few days of messages don't give you access to their whole life.
- **Reread the conversation just once**, without hunting for "what you did wrong". If you spot a real misstep, learn from it; otherwise, move on.
- **Avoid monitoring** their activity or checking again and again. It feeds the waiting instead of easing it.
- **Talk to someone close to you.** A friend, a cousin or a trusted colleague will quickly put things back in perspective.

## When to follow up, and how

Following up isn't a lack of pride. A **single message**, well chosen, is a grown-up move: it leaves the door open without forcing it.

The right moment depends on the rhythm you had. If you were chatting every day, a few days of silence are enough before following up. If your exchanges were more spaced out, let a little more time pass.

A few simple, light ways to put it:

- "Hi, I hope your week is going well. I was thinking about what you said about your home village, it made me smile."
- "I don't know if you're very busy at the moment, but I really enjoyed our chats. If you still feel like it, I'd be happy to carry on."
- "No problem if you'd rather leave it there, just let me know, it'll help me move on."

What is best avoided: reproaches ("You could have answered"), a burst of messages, a lone question mark, or sarcasm. They close the door instead of leaving it ajar.

## After the follow-up: accepting the silence

If your message gets no reply, you have your answer. It isn't graceful, but it is clear. **Don't follow up a second time**: at this point, insisting costs you more than it gives you.

Accepting doesn't mean approving. You can find this behaviour disappointing and still choose not to give it any more room in your days.

## Turning the page gently

A few concrete steps help close the chapter:

- **Archive the conversation in your mind.** There is no point reopening it to reread it at night.
- **Get back into your Discover routine** when you feel ready, gently, without trying to "make up for it".
- **Turn on Pause mode** if you need a few days to breathe. Your profile is no longer shown, and you come back whenever you want.
- **Look after yourself away from the screen**: an outing, some sport, a family meal. The real world often shrinks digital disappointments back to their true size.
- **Hold on to what was good.** If the conversations did you good, it proves you know how to build a connection. That is good news for what comes next.

## When the silence hides something else

Sometimes a disappearance follows behaviour that made you uncomfortable: pushiness, inappropriate remarks, a request for money. In that case, the silence is more of a relief. You can **block** the person so they can no longer write to you, and **report** them if their behaviour crossed the line. Both actions are always free, and the moderation team reviews every report.

## Don't do it to others

Almost all of us have been tempted, at some point, to vanish rather than say no. Yet we now know what that silence feels like on the other side. A few simple habits let you be the person who leaves gracefully:

- **A short message is enough.** "Thank you for our chats, I'd rather not take this further. I wish you lovely encounters."
- **No need for a long justification.** The longer the explanation, the more it invites debate.
- **Give a heads-up if you are taking a break.** "I'll be less available for a few weeks" saves the other person from worrying or doubting themselves.
- **Don't keep conversations "in reserve".** If you know the interest is gone, it is better to say so early.
- **Tell situations apart.** After two messages, not replying is acceptable. After several days of chatting or a date, a closing word is a mark of respect.

## What ghosting teaches us

Ghosting isn't an inevitable part of online dating. It simply reminds us that behind every profile is a person with their own clumsiness, fears and limits. It also reminds us that clarity is a gift: the clarity we receive, and the clarity we offer.

> A silence says nothing about your worth. It only says that this particular story has ended.

So if the silence settles in, allow yourself one follow-up, just one, kept light. Then let go. Meanwhile, other profiles are waiting for someone as attentive and sincere as you. And the day you want to end a conversation yourself, take thirty seconds to write it: someone, somewhere, will be grateful.
