When you start using a dating app, it is tempting to set the filters to the millimetre: a narrow age range, a single city, precise criteria. It feels like you are saving time. Then, after a few days, you barely see any profiles, and you can't understand why.

Filters are useful, but they are like salt: too little and everything is bland; too much and it is all you can taste. Here is how to get the balance right.

## What filters are really for

They are there to **rule out what clearly doesn't suit you**, not to describe the ideal person in advance. In other words: a filter is a safety rail, not an identikit portrait.

The most common criteria are:

- **Age**: the age range that suits you.
- **Location**: country, city, sometimes distance.
- **Intention**: serious relationship, marriage, dating, friendship.
- **Languages** and other preferences, depending on your plan.

Some finer filters are reserved for paid plans. The basic filters are more than enough to start with, and that is often a good starting point: you will soon see whether your problem is too few filters or, on the contrary, too many.

## Age: widen it by a few years

An age range that is too narrow is the most common reason lists run dry. If you are looking for "28 to 32", you exclude a 33-year-old you might like a lot, and you will never see their profile. Widening by two or three years on each side changes the quality of suggestions very little and clearly increases the number of profiles.

Keep the range narrow if it is a genuine life criterion (for example because you want to start a family within a specific timeframe), but ask yourself honestly whether the number is a need or a habit.

## Location: city, country and distance

Searching in your own city is natural: you can meet easily. But depending on where you live, the city alone may give you only a small number of profiles. A few options:

- **Widen to the region or the country** if you are willing to travel.
- **Include nearby towns**: in a big metropolitan area, the surrounding districts count as one living area.
- **Open up to other countries** if you are in a situation where distance is accepted (diaspora, work mobility, plans to move).

If you are open to a long-distance relationship, say so in your bio: people who see your profile will know what to expect.

## Intention: the filter to respect

Unlike age or location, intention is a criterion to **take seriously**. If you want a lasting relationship and you meet someone who only wants to chat, you will both waste time and energy. Conversely, if you would rather get to know people first without pressure, it is better to say so clearly than to let people believe you are looking for something more committed.

It is also the most honest criterion you can offer the other person. Update it when your situation changes: you can start with "I don't know yet" and be more specific later.

## The ideal-list trap

Filters encourage a tick-box mindset: height, job, religion, level of education. The more you add, the fewer profiles you see, and the closer you get to an imaginary portrait that doesn't exist.

A useful exercise: split your criteria into two columns.

- **Essential**: things without which a relationship cannot work (for example wanting children, the city, the intention).
- **Nice to have**: things that would be pleasant, but wouldn't stand in the way of a lovely story.

Use filters for the first column only. Let the second be checked in conversation: that is where you will see whether an "important" criterion really is.

## Reviewing when profiles run low

If your Discover page is emptying, that is a signal, not a failure. Go step by step:

1. **Widen the age range first** by a few years.
2. **Then extend the area** (a nearby town, the region).
3. **Review the secondary criteria**: which ones are truly essential?
4. **Wait a little**: new members join every day, especially in growing cities.

If, despite all that, the list stays thin, it may simply be that your city has few members for now. In that case, Crush Nights, Daily picks and suggestions from other cities give you another way to meet people.

## A concrete example

Awa, 29, lives in Abidjan. She had set her search to "men aged 30 to 33, Abidjan only, serious relationship". After a week, she kept seeing the same four or five profiles. She widened the age range to 28 to 37 and added the districts neighbouring Abidjan. The result: around fifteen new profiles a day, several of which surprised her, including a 35-year-old teacher she has been chatting with ever since. She didn't change what really mattered to her, intention and closeness, she simply made some room.

The lesson: a filter that is too tight doesn't protect you, it **deprives** you. When you are tempted to tighten it further, it is better to ask yourself what you are really trying to avoid. Often it isn't a number, it is a past experience. In that case, it is better to deal with it in conversation (by asking the right questions) than to pin it on a mechanical criterion.

## Filters and your own rhythm

One last point: filters don't have to be permanent. Many members adjust them according to their energy at the time. On an evening when you fancy something new, widen them. At a time when you want a very clear frame, tighten them. What you are looking for changes, and your search can follow.

## Don't forget the other direction

Your filters decide what **you** see. They don't decide who sees your profile. Your bio, your photos and your answers do the rest of the work. If you get few reactions, look at your profile too: an empty bio or an unclear photo often matters more than any filter.

## When to change, when to keep

- **Change** if you keep seeing the same profiles, or hardly see any at all.
- **Keep** if you regularly get relevant suggestions and haven't tried everything yet.
- **Test** a week with wider criteria, then compare: the quality of conversations is a better judge than the number of profiles.

> Good filters aren't there to find someone perfect, but to let enough people through for chance to do its work.

Set them once with care, review them every month, and keep in mind that the meeting happens in conversation, not in the boxes of a form.
