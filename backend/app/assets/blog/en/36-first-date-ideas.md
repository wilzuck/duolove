You've been chatting for a few days, the conversation flows, and at last the question comes: "Shall we meet?" That's great news... followed almost immediately by a small moment of panic. Where should you go? What can you suggest that isn't too ordinary, too intimidating or too expensive?

A good first date doesn't need to be spectacular. Above all, it should let you **talk, feel safe and leave whenever you want**. Here are some concrete ideas, designed with our cities in mind, to choose from according to your budget, the time of day and your personality.

## Three rules that apply to every idea

Before you even choose a place, keep these points in mind. They make any outing more relaxed:

- **A public, busy place.** No homes, no hotel rooms, nowhere isolated for a first meeting, even if the person seems lovely.
- **Your own way there and back.** Arrive by your own means and leave the same way. You stay in control of your timing.
- **Someone you trust in the loop.** Tell a trusted person where you're going, who with and until what time. On Crushduo, you can propose a date in the conversation and use **Share with someone I trust** to send the place and time to a loved one.

Add a reasonable length of time: an hour or two is enough. If it all goes well, you'll want to meet again, and that's exactly the point.

## The cafe or tea room: the great classic

It has proved itself for good reasons. A quiet cafe in the daytime offers everything you need:

- **Neutral ground**, where nobody feels out of place.
- **A noise level that lets you talk** without raising your voice.
- **An easy exit**: a coffee comes to a natural end, without awkwardness.

It's ideal if you're on the reserved side, if you'd like a short meeting, or if one of you has a busy day. A break in the late afternoon, before the city comes alive, works very well.

## The maquis or neighbourhood restaurant

For those who love atmosphere, a well-run **maquis** (a local open-air eatery) or a small, well-known restaurant is a warm option. Sharing grilled fish, alloco (fried plantain) or rice with peanut sauce is already a way of getting to know each other: what the other person likes, how they order, how kind they are to the waiter.

A few tips to keep it pleasant:

- **Pick a place you know**, where you can hear each other talk. Avoid big match nights or very loud music.
- **Go for early evening** rather than a late night out.
- **Talk about the bill openly.** Each pays their share, one of you treats the other, you take turns next time: what matters is that nobody feels indebted. An invitation is a gesture, never a debt.

## The seafront or the riverbank

In Cotonou, Lomé, Abidjan or Dakar, the beach and seafront promenades are natural meeting places. Elsewhere, the banks of a river or lake play the same role. Walking side by side takes away a lot of pressure: you're not sitting face to face searching for words, you look at the view and comment on whatever goes by.

- **Stay in lively areas**, near drinks stalls, families and other walkers.
- **Choose the late afternoon**, when the heat drops and the light is beautiful, and head home before it's fully dark.
- **Have a fallback spot**: a beach shack where you can sit down for a drink if the walk goes on.

It's a lovely idea for people who find it easier to talk while moving.

## The craft market or cultural centre

Wandering through a **craft market**, among fabrics, carvings, jewellery and basketwork, gives you endless things to talk about. You discover the other person's tastes, their memories ("my grandmother used to weave this kind of cloth"), their curiosity. **Cultural centres**, photo or painting **exhibitions** and **museums** offer the same advantage in a calmer setting.

These outings suit:

- **curious personalities**, who love learning and commenting;
- **tight budgets**, since many exhibitions are free or cheap;
- **daytime dates**, often on a Saturday morning or Sunday afternoon.

One tip: end the visit with a drink somewhere quiet, so you can carry on talking sitting down.

## The public garden or park

A botanical garden, a shady park, the square in front of a monument: these simple places have a quiet charm. You can sit on a bench, share a bissap (hibiscus) or ginger juice bought nearby, and talk with no time pressure.

It's a gentle option for a **daytime** first date, if you'd rather avoid alcohol, if you're a calm person, or if you want a meeting that's very easy on the budget.

## By budget, time of day and personality

To help you choose quickly:

- **Small budget**: a seafront walk, a public garden, a craft market, a free exhibition, a juice from a stall.
- **Medium budget**: a cafe, a tea room, a neighbourhood maquis, an ice-cream parlour.
- **In the morning**: a market, a garden, a weekend cafe.
- **In the late afternoon**: the seafront, a terrace, a cultural centre.
- **In the early evening**: a maquis or lively restaurant, always in a neighbourhood you know.
- **If you're shy**: an activity that keeps your hands and eyes busy (an exhibition, a market, a walk) makes talking easier.
- **If you're very chatty**: a quiet cafe will give you time to listen as much as you talk.

## What's best avoided at first

Some ideas are excellent... for later. For a first meeting, set aside:

- **The cinema**: two hours in the dark without talking isn't ideal for getting to know each other.
- **Outings far from town**, or anything that means getting into the other person's vehicle.
- **Invitations to someone's home**, either way.
- **Very late nights**, when getting home alone becomes complicated.
- **Meeting in front of one person's whole group of friends**, which puts the other under examination.

If the person pushes hard to change the venue, come to your place or pick you up, that's useful information. You can always **report or block** a profile that makes you uncomfortable: it's free and immediate.

## Before you go, one last word

On the day, send a quick message to confirm the time and place. Arrive on time with a charged phone, and give yourself permission to cut it short if the atmosphere doesn't feel right. A polite "thank you for this, I'm going to head off" is always enough.

> The best place for a date isn't the most impressive one, it's the one where you can be yourself without watching the clock.

Keep it simple, keep it safe, and let the conversation do the rest. A coffee shared in the shade or a walk facing the sea can become the start of a beautiful story. It's up to you to take the first step.
