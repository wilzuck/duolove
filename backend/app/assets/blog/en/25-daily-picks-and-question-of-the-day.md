There are two ways to use a dating app. The first is to stay on it for hours, scrolling through profiles until the faces blur together. The second is to spend a short moment on it, at regular intervals, with real attention. The second is far less tiring, and it often gives better results.

**Daily picks** and the **Question of the day** are made for this second approach. They're small daily appointments that curb the urge to "see everything" and bring your attention back to just a few people.

## Daily picks: few profiles, but chosen ones

Every day, you're offered a short selection of profiles. How many depends on your plan: fewer on Free, more with a paid plan. These profiles are chosen for you, based on what you've filled in, not at random.

The point of this limit is psychological before it's technical. When you have five profiles in front of you, you really look at them: you read the bio, look at all the photos, read the answers. When you have five hundred, you move at the speed of a thumb that decides in a second. Fewer profiles means more attention, and more attention means better decisions.

### How to go through them

- **Pick a fixed moment.** In the morning with your coffee, or in the evening before bed. A ritual takes root better than a vague habit.
- **Read before you decide.** Take thirty seconds per profile: photos, bio, a detail that draws you in.
- **Respond with something specific.** If a profile appeals to you, a Like with a short note about a detail makes all the difference.
- **Accept passing.** Saying no is part of the game. A profile that doesn't speak to you deserves neither guilt nor long deliberation.

## The Question of the day: a small door to the other person

The Question of the day is a light question, the same for everyone, which you answer by choosing from a few options. It works as a "group icebreaker": an effortless way to learn something about yourself and others, and to spot things in common.

It works well because it asks you neither to expose yourself nor to think for long. In a few seconds, you express a preference. And when someone you're discovering has answered the same way as you, that small thing in common creates instant warmth, however tiny. It isn't a serious compatibility test; it's a **shared smile**.

### How to make the most of it

- **Answer spontaneously**, without looking for the answer that "looks good". The whole point is sincerity.
- **Use it to start a conversation**: "I saw we picked the same answer to the Question of the day, that's rare!"
- **Don't look for hidden meaning.** Someone who answered differently isn't incompatible: they just like something else.

## The mistakes that wear you out

A few habits turn this ritual into a chore:

- **Coming back every ten minutes.** If you're waiting for a reply, opening the app every five minutes speeds nothing up. Set your notifications so you're alerted, and close the app the rest of the time.
- **Liking everyone for fear of missing someone.** It fills your inbox with duos you don't really want, and forces you to sort through them afterwards.
- **Comparing.** "She's not as good as yesterday's." Each profile deserves to be read on its own terms, not against the previous ones.
- **Trying to reach the end.** There's no end to the list of possible people. Setting yourself a number of profiles to look at protects you from that illusion.

## A ten-minute ritual

Here's a routine that fits into a coffee break:

1. **Open your Daily picks** and read the suggested profiles without rushing.
2. **Answer the Question of the day**, in a second.
3. **Send one or two Likes** with a short, sincere note, only to people who genuinely made you want to.
4. **Reply to ongoing conversations**, rather than opening new ones.
5. **Close the app.** You'll be back tomorrow.

Ten minutes is enough. The rest of the day belongs to your life, which remains the best place to build a relationship.

## An example of an ordinary week

To make this concrete, here's the rhythm of Yves, 34, who works days in Lomé:

- **Monday to Thursday**, ten minutes in the morning: Daily picks, Question of the day, two replies to ongoing conversations.
- **Friday evening**, an extra quarter of an hour to reply without rushing and suggest a call to someone he enjoys chatting with.
- **At the weekend**, he leaves the app alone, except for a Duo Night if he feels like it.

After a month, he'd had three real conversations, one of which led to a coffee. He never spent more than an hour a week on the app, and never felt he was "on call". It isn't a model to copy exactly: it's proof that a regular, brief presence is often enough.

## What if nothing comes up?

Sometimes your Daily picks don't inspire you for several days. Before concluding that the app doesn't work, check three things: is your profile complete (photos, bio, interests)? Are your filters too tight? Does your city have enough active members for now? Adjust what you can, then give it a few days. New members arrive every week, and the daily selection is constantly renewed.

## When your rhythm changes

There are periods when you don't have the energy for a daily ritual: intense work, tiredness, bereavement, moving house. At those times, rather than forcing yourself, use Pause mode or cut back to two or three visits a week. An app that bores you won't help you meet anyone.

On the other hand, some evenings you'll want to spend more time: a lively conversation, a Duo Night, simple curiosity. Go ahead without guilt. The aim isn't to impose a rigid rule on yourself, but to **stay in control** of the time you give the app.

## What the ritual changes over time

Over a month, this small daily habit has several effects:

- you **read profiles better**, so you choose more wisely;
- you **reply faster** to the people you're chatting with, because you're consistent;
- you **get less tired**, because you aren't swallowed up by scrolling;
- you **keep perspective**, and don't let your mood depend on how many reactions you receive.

> A dating app should feel like an appointment with yourself rather than a waiting room.

Daily picks and the Question of the day aren't gimmicks: they're safeguards against excess. Use them as a small daily appointment, and save the rest of your energy for what matters: the people who write to you, and life away from the screen.
