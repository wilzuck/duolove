Every feature that promises to "get you noticed" stirs up the same small temptation: what if I used it all the time? What if, by pushing hard enough, the right person eventually stumbled on me? That reasoning has a flaw: it turns meeting someone into a visibility contest, and nobody wants to win that kind of contest.

Super Likes and Boosts are tools. They're useful at specific moments, and they replace neither a good profile nor genuine curiosity. Let's look at when they make sense, when they don't, and how to keep them in their place.

## Two tools, two different gestures

A **Super Like** is a targeted gesture: it's aimed at one particular person. Your profile gets priority with them, and they're invited to respond. A **Boost**, on the other hand, is a gesture aimed at everyone: your profile is given temporary visibility, in front of more people for a while.

The difference is easy to sum up. A Super Like says "it's you I see". A Boost says "I'm here, this is my profile". One is a personal message, the other a lit-up shop window.

Neither guarantees a reply, a match or a conversation. They change how you're seen, not what the other person feels when they read. Keep that sentence in mind: it saves a lot of disappointment.

## When a Super Like is a lovely gesture

A Super Like is valuable precisely because it's rare. If you use it on every profile you like a little, it becomes noise. If you save it for the ones that really stop you in your tracks, it gets its meaning back.

A few situations where it's justified:

- **A real coincidence.** You read that she likes walking at dawn before work, and that's exactly your habit. You don't want that detail to go unnoticed.
- **A very popular profile.** When someone gets a lot of attention, a clearer signal can help you not get lost in the crowd, without any claim that it's sure to work.
- **A hesitation to get past.** You'd have sent a Like, but something tells you to commit a bit more.

In every case, have a real reason in your head, even if you don't write it down. If you can't find what you like about this person, a simple Like was enough.

## When a Like with a short note is the best choice

This is the feature people underrate, even though it's often the most effective. A Like with a short note is a brief sentence, sent with your very first gesture. It tells the other person you've read their profile.

Take Armand, who's torn between a Super Like and a Like with a note for Salimata. In one of her profile questions, she mentioned that she hates awkward silences at restaurants. Armand writes: "Do you like silences that speak or silences that weigh? I have a soft spot for the first kind." That beats a thousand priority signals: the note is unique, it couldn't have been sent to anyone else.

Choose a Like with a short note when:

1. you have something specific to say about the other person's profile;
2. you'd rather let them reply at their own pace, without pressure;
3. you don't want to feel committed to anything beyond simple curiosity.

A Super Like catches the eye, a short note catches the attention. They're not the same thing.

## What a Boost actually is

A Boost puts your profile in the spotlight for a limited time. Picture a sign at the entrance to a market: it doesn't sell anything for you, it just makes more passers-by look up.

That visibility doesn't change you. If your profile is empty, blurry or rushed, more people will see it, but no more people will find it attractive. A Boost amplifies what's already there. That's why it only makes sense on a profile you're proud to show.

## When a Boost makes sense

Two moments really suit it.

**During a Crush Night.** These evenings bring together people who are online at the same time and want to chat there and then. It's the setting where extra visibility pays off directly: more people present, more chances of crossing paths with someone who wouldn't otherwise have seen your profile. It doesn't guarantee anyone will write to you, but your profile is in the room.

**With a new, complete profile.** You've just finished signing up: photos approved by moderation, profile questions answered, values chosen, maybe the verified badge earned. You've taken care over how you present yourself, so it may as well be seen while it's at its freshest.

On the other hand, a Boost on a half-finished profile is a waste. Complete it first, boost it afterwards.

## Don't turn it into a race

This is the most important point, and it isn't about money but about mindset.

The race begins when you start watching. You send a Super Like and refresh the screen. You switch on a Boost and count the visits. Every missing reply becomes a score. Very quickly, you're no longer meeting anyone: you're measuring your worth.

To avoid this, a few simple habits:

- **Decide before you tap** what you'll do if nothing happens. The answer is usually: nothing special, you carry on as before.
- **Leave your phone alone** while a Boost is running. Do something else, come back later, see what's arrived.
- **Keep your other gestures alive.** Answering the Question of the day, browsing your Daily picks, starting a conversation: these count just as much as a paid option.
- **Don't compare.** One person's visibility says nothing about another's worth.

If the waiting starts to weigh on you, using Pause mode is a right, not a failure.

## What no option guarantees

Let's be clear. No Super Like guarantees a reply. No Boost guarantees messages, a match or a relationship. The people on the other side have their own tastes, availability and mood of the day. An option puts you in their line of sight; what they do with it is up to them.

You also have to accept the reverse: a reply doesn't always come from a visible gesture. Many lovely conversations start with a simple Like, or with a reaction to a message in a chat where you've already been talking. Visibility helps you be seen, never chosen.

## A sensible budget

The paid plans, Premium, VIP and Gold, are paid for once, by mobile money, with no automatic renewal: you stay in control of what you spend. For options that put you forward, the useful rule is a ceiling set in advance.

A good habit: decide, before opening the app, how much you're willing to spend on these options this month. Once you've reached it, that's it, no guilt and no "just one last time". That limit protects your budget, but above all your mood.

Ask yourself too: is this money bringing me closer to meeting someone, or to a feeling of control? If it's the second, you're better off without it. A well-written profile and a sincere short note cost less and often do better.

## A simple way to find your bearings

Ask yourself three questions before each option.

1. **Do I know why this person interests me?** If yes, a Super Like or a Like with a short note. If not, wait.
2. **Is my profile ready to be seen?** If yes, a Boost can make sense, especially during a Crush Night. If not, complete it first.
3. **Can I let it run its course?** If you know the waiting will eat at you, save the option for another day.

Standing out, in the end, is first about being readable: a profile that feels like a real person, a note that could only have been written for one person. Options come afterwards, like a light shone on something that already exists.
