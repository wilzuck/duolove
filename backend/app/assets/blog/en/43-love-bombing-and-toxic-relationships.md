At first, it is exhilarating. Messages from morning to night, compliments on every photo, plans for the future after just a few days. You feel unique, chosen, finally understood. Then, little by little, something changes: attention becomes surveillance, tenderness turns into reproach, and you catch yourself walking on eggshells.

This pattern has a name: **love bombing**. It isn't tied to any gender or background, and it can happen to anyone. This guide helps you recognise the signs, understand what is going on and protect yourself, without becoming suspicious of everyone.

## What is love bombing?

Love bombing means overwhelming someone with attention, declarations and promises from the very start of a relationship. Enthusiasm in itself is nothing suspicious. The problem is the **disproportionate intensity** and, above all, what comes next: once the attachment is in place, affection becomes a means of pressure.

The difference from a genuine crush comes down to one question: **does this person respect your pace?** Sincere interest adapts to you. Love bombing imposes its own speed.

## The signs of love bombing

- **Very fast declarations.** "You're the love of my life", "I've never felt like this" after just a few chats.
- **An overwhelming presence.** Dozens of messages a day, and the expectation of an immediate reply.
- **Rushed plans**: marriage, children, living together, meeting the family, when you have never even met.
- **Gifts or promises** that create a feeling of debt.
- **Excessive idealisation.** You are "perfect", "different from everyone else", which makes any future disappointment all the more brutal.

## When attention turns into control

The beginnings of a toxic relationship often creep in gently. Here are the behaviours to watch for.

### Control

- **Wanting to know where you are at all times**, asking for proof, photos, locations.
- **Demanding access to your phone** or your conversations.
- **Making decisions for you**: your clothes, your outings, who you spend time with.

### Isolation

- **Criticising the people close to you**: "Your sister is jealous of us", "Your friends don't want what's best for you."
- **Blaming you for time spent elsewhere**, at work, with family, at the mosque, at church or with friends.
- **Asking you to keep secrets** about the relationship or certain conversations.

### Guilt-tripping

- **Turning every complaint around**: you express discomfort, and you are the one who ends up apologising.
- **Using silence as punishment**, then coming back full of tenderness as if nothing had happened.
- **Dismissing your feelings**: "You're exaggerating", "You're too sensitive."

### Emotional blackmail

- "If you really loved me, you'd do this for me."
- "If you leave, I'll never get over it."
- Veiled threats, or requests for money presented as proof of love.

Not all of these signs need to be present. **Two or three, repeatedly, are enough reason to slow down.**

## Why you don't see it straight away

Love bombing works because it answers a deep need: to be loved, recognised, chosen. The first weeks leave such a strong memory that you then try to get that feeling back, even if it means excusing what isn't right. Add to that, sometimes, social pressure around marriage, the fear of being alone or what the people around you might think.

There is nothing naive about having been swept away. **The responsibility lies with the person who manipulates**, not with the person who trusted.

## Protecting yourself from the first messages

A few simple habits limit the risks:

- **Keep your own pace.** You have every right to say: "I enjoy our chats, but I need to take things slowly." The other person's reaction will tell you a lot.
- **Stay on the app's messaging** at first. You keep the option to **block** and **report** at any time, for free.
- **Favour verified profiles.** The **Verified profile badge** means a moderator has compared a selfie with the profile photos. It doesn't guarantee intentions, but it confirms the person looks like their photos.
- **Don't share too quickly** your address, your workplace or intimate photos.
- **Never send money.** If a message looks like a request for money, Crushduo shows an **automatic warning** under that message: take it seriously.
- **For a first meeting**, **propose the date in the app** and use "Share with someone I trust" to send the place and time to someone close to you, and choose a public spot.

## Talking about it, without shame

Isolation is the ally of toxic relationships. Simply talking about it often changes your perspective:

- **Choose someone you trust**: a brother, a friend, an aunt, a colleague.
- **Describe the facts, not just your feelings**: what was said, asked, held against you.
- **Listen to their outside view**, even if it is unsettling. The people close to us sometimes see what attachment hides from us.

If someone close to you comes to you, listen without judging. A sentence like "you should have seen it coming" closes the door instead of opening it.

## Asking for help

If the situation worries you, if you feel threatened or if there has been violence, you don't have to deal with it alone:

- **Block and report** the profile from the conversation. The moderation team reviews every report.
- **Keep the messages**: they can serve as evidence.
- **Lean on the people around you**, and on community or religious leaders you trust.
- **Turn to victim support organisations** in your country or the city where you live.
- **If you are in danger, contact the authorities**: the police, the gendarmerie or emergency services.

## Choosing a relationship that does you good

A healthy relationship doesn't ask you to justify yourself all the time. It leaves room for the people you love, your work, your dreams. It accepts your "no" and waits for your "yes".

> True love doesn't cut you off from the world: it walks through it with you.

If something makes you uncomfortable, trust that signal. Slow down, talk about it, protect yourself. And remember that sincere, patient and respectful people exist, on Crushduo as everywhere else. They deserve your trust, and you deserve a relationship where you feel safe.
