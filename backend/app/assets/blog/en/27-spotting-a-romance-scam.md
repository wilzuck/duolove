Almost everyone thinks "it only happens to other people". Yet romance scams work precisely because they don't look like scams: they look like a beautiful story moving fast, with a caring person who writes every day. Then the trap closes, often with a request that seems almost reasonable.

This guide isn't trying to make you suspicious of everyone. It gives you clear markers to recognise the patterns, keep a cool head and protect both your money and your heart.

## The typical script, in four acts

The details change, but the mechanics are very often the same:

1. **Fast-tracked seduction.** Compliments from the very first message, "love at first sight" within days, words of love very early on.
2. **Moving you away from the app.** You are quickly asked to continue on another messaging app, to share your number, sometimes to stop using the app altogether.
3. **The dramatic life story.** A sick parent, a contract abroad, a parcel stuck somewhere, an inheritance to release, an accident.
4. **The request.** Money, airtime, a code, customs or shipping fees, a "small favour" that turns out to be expensive.

Everything can stop before the fourth act if you recognise the first three.

## The most reliable warning signs

- **Someone who can never show their face.** No video call, always an excuse (bad network, broken camera, working abroad). A real person almost always finds a way to see you, even briefly.
- **A profile that is too good, a story that is too perfect.** Magazine-style photos, a prestigious job, a life abroad, a widow or widower with a child: a portrait straight out of a novel.
- **Very fast declarations of love**, which make what comes next hard to refuse.
- **An emergency that forces you to decide quickly.** "I only have tonight", "it has to be paid today". Urgency is the main weapon: it stops you from thinking and from talking to anyone about it.
- **Requests for secrecy.** "Don't tell your family, they wouldn't understand."
- **A story that keeps changing.** Details that contradict each other, names that vary, times that don't add up.
- **A preference for payment methods that are hard to trace**: mobile money transfers, gift cards, cryptocurrency, money orders.

One of these signs on its own proves nothing. Several together should make you **slow down immediately**.

## Phrases to watch out for

Some messages come back almost word for word from one scam to the next:

- "I need your help, just a little something to tide me over."
- "Can you send me some money by mobile money? I'll pay you back tomorrow."
- "My parcel is stuck at customs, the fees have to be paid."
- "Buy an airtime top-up card and send me the code."
- "I want to send you a gift, but the shipping has to be paid first."
- "Message me on WhatsApp, it's easier and more private."

On Crushduo, a message that looks like a request for money or an invitation to leave the platform may trigger a **warning** shown under the message. It doesn't block anything and doesn't decide for you: it simply reminds you never to send money to someone you haven't met. Take it as a brake, not an accusation.

## The golden rule: no money, ever

It is simple, and there are no exceptions: **never send money, airtime or a code to someone you haven't met in person**, whatever the story. A sincere relationship isn't measured by what you agree to pay, and someone who loves you will never put you in a position where saying no feels like betrayal.

If the request makes you uncomfortable, that alone is a strong enough signal. You don't have to justify yourself or "prove" your trust.

## How to check, without accusing

If you have doubts, a few simple steps can help you see more clearly, without a fight:

- **Suggest a video call**, even a short one. Repeated refusals speak volumes.
- **Do a reverse image search** on their photos: if they show up under other names, you have your answer.
- **Ask simple questions about everyday life**, and notice whether the answers stay consistent from one day to the next.
- **Talk to someone you trust.** An outside view often sees what attachment hides.
- **Take your time.** Don't respond to any urgent request before you have slept on it.

## What to do if you have doubts

1. **Stop discussing** the subject of the request. You don't owe any explanation.
2. **Pay nothing**, not even "a small amount just to see".
3. **Keep the messages** as evidence: don't delete them.
4. **Report the profile** from the conversation, choosing the right reason: the moderation team can act, including to protect other members.
5. **Block** the person if they insist or put pressure on you.

## What to do if you have already sent money

First of all, **don't blame yourself**: these scams are designed by professional manipulators. Shame is exactly what stops people from asking for help.

- **Contact your mobile operator or your bank immediately**: a very recent transfer can sometimes be flagged or stopped.
- **Report the situation** to the platform and, depending on your country, to the authorities that handle cybercrime.
- **Don't send anything more**, including to "recover" money already paid: that is a very common second scam.
- **Talk to someone you trust.** You are not alone, and you are not the first.

## Share this with the people close to you

The people most at risk are often those who don't dare to talk about it. A simple conversation with a parent, a brother or a friend before you start dating online changes a lot: tell them about the signs above and agree that you will show them any request for money, however small. Many scams end right there: shame and secrecy are their best allies, and talking is their worst enemy. If someone close to you shares a doubt, listen without judging: you don't help anyone by asking "how could you believe that?".

## Keeping your trust despite it all

Knowing all this shouldn't stop you from meeting people. The vast majority of members are here for the right reasons. The idea is to **go slow on what costs** (money, secrets, personal information) and **go fast on what reassures** (a video call, meeting in a public place, the opinion of someone close to you).

> An honest person always understands when you take your time. A dishonest one never does.

Keep these markers in mind, share them with your loved ones, and if a doubt appears, listen to it: it is almost always right.
