A fake profile is every dating app's nightmare: stolen photos, a made-up name, a story written to extract money or attention. To tackle this, Crushduo offers **selfie verification**. Verified profiles display a small badge next to their name.

The badge is useful, but it doesn't work miracles. To use it wisely, you need to understand what it proves, what it doesn't, and how to get it without stress.

## How it works

The principle is simple: you send a selfie, following the instructions shown in the app. A member of the team compares that selfie with your profile photos. If they match, the badge is granted. If the selfie is blurry, badly framed or doesn't allow a comparison, the request is declined and you can send another one.

This review is **human**, not just automatic: that's what makes it more reliable against crude edits, and it's also why the answer can take a little time.

### Tips to get it right first time

- **Natural light**, face clearly visible, no sunglasses, no cap hiding your eyes.
- **The same look as in your photos**: if you've changed your hairstyle or beard, also add a recent photo to your profile.
- **Simple framing**: face centred, looking straight ahead, no filters or effects.
- **A plain background**: no need for a backdrop, but avoid strong backlight.
- **Consistent profile photos**: verification compares your selfie with your profile photos. If those photos hardly ever show you, or you're always seen from behind, the comparison becomes difficult.

## What the badge proves

The badge shows that **the person who created the profile looks like the person in the photos**, at the time of verification. That's valuable information:

- it rules out most profiles that use stolen photos;
- it makes **large-scale fraud** less profitable, since it requires a real face and a human review;
- it shows the person has agreed to **make a small effort at transparency**, which is already a sign of seriousness.

## What the badge doesn't prove

This is the part not to forget:

- It **doesn't prove the person's honesty**: a real person can still lie about their situation, age or intentions.
- It **doesn't check** job, family situation, marital status or income.
- It **doesn't guarantee the safety** of a date: someone can be verified and still mean harm.
- It **says nothing about the future**: a verification done months ago doesn't stop an account from being misused later.

In other words, the badge reduces one specific risk (the fake face). It doesn't replace the caution you apply to any meeting.

## How to read a verified profile

A few good habits:

1. **See the badge as a good starting point**, not a final green light.
2. **Keep observing**: consistency between the photos, the bio and the way someone writes remains an excellent indicator.
3. **Keep your safety rules**: never give money, don't share sensitive information, and plan a first date in a public place.
4. **Report anything that worries you**, verified or not. Problematic behaviour is still problematic, even on a profile with a badge.

## What if you're not comfortable with verification?

That's completely understandable. Here are a few common reasons, and what can be said about them:

- **"I don't want to show my face to an app."** Verification exists precisely to stop fake profiles from circulating. If your concern is privacy, read the information shown when you send the selfie to find out how it's used, and ask support your questions before you go ahead.
- **"I'm not photogenic."** The comparison doesn't judge your looks: it only checks that it really is you.
- **"My situation is unusual."** If you're in a situation where showing your face is a risk (a sensitive profession, for example), write to support: it's better to ask than to give up in silence.

Verification is **optional**. A profile without a badge isn't suspicious in itself, but it's normal that some people prefer to chat with verified profiles first.

## After verification

Once you have the badge, a few simple precautions keep it meaningful:

- **Keep your photos up to date.** If your appearance changes a lot (haircut, beard, significant weight loss or gain), add a recent photo: the goal is that the person who meets you recognises you.
- **Don't share your account.** The badge is tied to you; lending it to someone else, even someone close, destroys its meaning.
- **Stay alert yourself.** A badge doesn't excuse you from reading other people's profiles with common sense: verifying a face doesn't verify an intention.

## What the team does on its side

Behind a badge, there are people looking at selfies and comparing them with photos, which takes rigour and respect. The images sent for verification are used for that purpose and nothing else; if you have any doubts about how they're handled, the privacy policy and support are there to answer. And if a request is declined, it isn't a judgement on you: it's usually a lighting or framing issue, fixed by trying again.

## Why it helps everyone

A badge benefits both the person who gets it and the person who reads it:

- for the verified member, it **increases trust** and makes first exchanges easier;
- for the reader, it **cuts down the time spent doubting**;
- for the community, it makes scams **more costly** to organise.

In some configurations, verification can also unlock benefits, for example a discount for verified profiles when subscribing. These benefits depend on the period and the settings in force: check the subscription page for the current conditions.

## In short

> A badge isn't a guarantee of good faith, it's the beginning of proof: "this person really is the one in the photos".

Get it if it suits you, use it as one clue among others, and keep your common sense. A successful meeting always rests on the same things: an honest conversation, an unhurried pace and a first date in a place where you feel at ease.
