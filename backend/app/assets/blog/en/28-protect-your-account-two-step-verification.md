Your dating account says a lot about you: your photos, your private conversations, your preferences, your city, sometimes your purchases. If someone else takes control of it, they can read your messages, pretend to be you with people who trust you, or spend your tokens. Protecting your account isn't a technical detail: it is protecting your privacy.

The good news is that a few simple steps, done once, keep you safe from the vast majority of attacks.

## Your password: the foundation

A good password doesn't have to be complicated, but it must be **long and unique**.

- **Length first.** At least ten characters with one capital letter, one lowercase letter and one number is the minimum required. Longer is better: a phrase of several words that only you know is stronger than a short word stuffed with symbols.
- **One password per service.** If you reuse the same one everywhere, a leak on a poorly secured site gives access to all your accounts.
- **Nothing guessable.** No date of birth, no first name, no name of a loved one, no "123456".
- **A password manager** can help you have long, different passwords without memorising them. Failing that, a notebook kept somewhere safe is better than a password that is too simple.

Never give your password to anyone, not even someone claiming to be from support: the Crushduo team will never ask you for it.

## Two-step verification: the real lock

Even an excellent password can be stolen (a fake sign-in page, a leak, someone looking over your shoulder). **Two-step verification** adds a second barrier: after your password, the app asks for a six-digit code that changes every 30 seconds and that only your phone can generate.

In practice, a thief who knows your password is stuck: they don't have your phone.

### How to turn it on

It is optional, and you set it up in your account's security settings:

1. Install an **authenticator app** on your phone (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy, FreeOTP…).
2. In Crushduo, ask to turn it on: a **setup key** appears, along with a link that opens the app directly on your phone.
3. Add the account in the authenticator app, then **enter the code shown** to confirm everything works.
4. Write down your **backup codes** (see below), which are shown only once.

It takes about two minutes. After that, each time you sign in, you enter your password (or use the link sent by email, or Google), then the code from the app.

### No SMS to pay for, no network needed

The app generates the code **on your phone**, offline. So you need neither SMS credit nor a network signal at sign-in time to get the code. It is a good solution where mobile coverage is patchy.

## Backup codes: your insurance

What happens if you lose your phone? That is what **backup codes** are for: around ten single-use codes, shown only once when you turn the feature on.

- **Keep them off your phone**: on paper stored at home, or in a password manager.
- **Each code works only once.** When you are running low, generate new ones.
- **Don't take a photo of them** in your gallery, which anyone who unlocks your phone can see.

If you lose both your phone and your codes, contact support: the team can reset two-step verification after checking your identity, which signs out all your devices as a precaution.

## The alert for a sign-in from another country

When a sign-in to your account is detected from a different country than the previous one, you receive a **notification** and an **email**. It doesn't block anything: a trip, a connection through a foreign network or time away studying are perfectly normal reasons.

But if it wasn't you:

1. **Change your password immediately**.
2. **Sign out of other devices** from the list of connected devices.
3. **Turn on two-step verification** if you haven't already.
4. If you see messages you didn't write, report it to support.

Worth knowing: your IP address is never stored for this, only the country of the sign-in.

## Everyday habits

- **Beware of fake links.** A sign-in page that arrives in an unexpected message or email may be a trap: type the site address yourself or use your installed app.
- **Don't sign in on a shared computer** without signing out afterwards. Avoid ticking "Stay signed in" in a cybercafé.
- **Keep your phone and its browser up to date**: many attacks exploit flaws that have already been fixed.
- **Lock your phone** with a code or a fingerprint.
- **Check your connected devices from time to time** and sign out of any you don't recognise.

## What if you sign in with Google?

If you use Google to sign in, you don't have a separate Crushduo password: your security therefore depends heavily on your Google account. Turn on 2-Step Verification at Google and, if you wish, also add Crushduo's two-step verification: it will then be required whatever sign-in method you use. To turn it off later, a code is enough (you have no password to provide).

## A habit worth building: the yearly security check-up

Once a year, take ten minutes to go over your account:

1. **Change your password** if you have been using it for a long time or if you have typed it on a device you don't control.
2. **Check the list of connected devices** and sign out of any you no longer recognise.
3. **Check your backup codes**: do you have enough left? Are they still stored in the same place?
4. **Review your privacy settings**: who can see your activity, your profile, your approximate location.

It is quick, and it turns security from a vague worry into a concrete task, like getting a car's oil checked.

## In short

> A well-protected account comes down to three things: a long, unique password, two-step verification, and backup codes kept in the right place.

It is a ten-minute investment that saves you months of trouble. And as always with security, the best time to deal with it is before you need it.
